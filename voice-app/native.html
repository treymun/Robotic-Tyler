Overview

How Tyler reaches your Steam voice room

No embedded browser and no Steam injection. Tyler plays speech into a Windows virtual audio cable, and Steam uses the other end of that cable as its microphone.

  1. TylerAI reply as speech
  2. CABLE InputTyler's output device

Steam text chat

Not read here. Use the Control Center or the Chrome extension for chat features.

Audio devices

Choose CABLE Input for Tyler. In Steam, open Settings → Voice and choose CABLE Output as the microphone. They're the two ends of the same virtual cable.

To send your own voice too, route your microphone into the same cable with a mixer such as Voicemeeter. This app never controls or intercepts Steam's input.

Talk through Steam

Listening records everything playing on the selected output device, not only Steam. Choose the speakers or headphones Steam uses, not the virtual cable, and get everyone's consent first.

Which app does what

All three programs share the same settings design. Pick the one that matches what you want Tyler to do.

Chrome extension
The most complete option: Steam web voice and chat, the soundboard, music, party host, trivia and moderation.
Control Center
Text-only Steam chat in its own window, with moderation, trivia, party tools and settings.
Native Steam companion
This app. Routes AI speech into the real Steam client through a virtual cable, with optional short listening. It doesn't read Steam text chat.

First-time setup

  1. Install VB-CABLE from VB-Audio and restart Windows if it asks.
  2. Open Personality & API settings and add your OpenRouter key.
  3. In Voice studio, choose CABLE Input as Tyler's output device.
  4. In Steam, open Settings → Voice and choose CABLE Output as your microphone.
  5. Join a group voice room and press Play test tone. Ask a friend whether they heard it.
  6. Type a message or use the short listening option to talk through Steam.

A selected device doesn't prove Steam is connected, so always test with a friend. Recording PC audio requires everyone's consent.
